A: 请问，您贵姓？🔊(Qǐng wèn, nín guì xìng?) - May I ask your surname?

语法

I. 您 (nín)

"您 (nín)" is the polite form of the second person singular "你 (nǐ)". It is typically used when addressing elders or individuals with a higher social status.

For example: 李老师 (Lǐ lǎoshī) (Teacher Li), 您好 (nínhǎo)!

When meeting someone for the first time, even if they are of similar age or status, "您 (nín)" can be used to express politeness and courtesy.

Example: Two people (who are about the same age) meeting for the first time

A: 您好 (Nínhǎo)！ (Hello! (polite))
B: 您好 (Nínhǎo)！ (Hello! (polite))
II. 您贵姓 (nín guì xìng)

"贵 (guì)" means "noble" or "honored." Therefore, "您贵姓 (nín guì xìng)" is a polite way to ask for someone's surname. When answering, you typically state your surname or full name.

您贵姓 (nín guì xìng)？

Note: You should not use "贵姓 (guì xìng)" to refer to your own surname.

A: 请问 (Qǐng wèn)，您贵姓 (nín guì xìng)？ - May I ask your surname?
B: 我姓李 (Wǒ xìng Lǐ)。 - My surname is Li.
A: 请问 (Qǐng wèn)，您贵姓 (nín guì xìng)？ - May I ask your surname?
B: 我姓李 (Wǒ xìng Lǐ)。我叫李文中 (Wǒ jiào Lǐ Wénzhōng)。 - My surname is Li. My full name is Wenzhong Li.
III. Interrogative Pronouns 什么 (shénme) and 谁 (shéi)

"什么 (shénme)" as an interrogative pronoun can be used alone to mean "What?". More commonly, it is placed before a noun to ask for specific information.

你叫什么名字 (nǐ jiào shénme míngzi)？

The word order in Chinese questions with interrogative pronouns generally follows the same structure as affirmative sentences, with the question word placed where the answer would be.

"谁 (shéi)" is another interrogative pronoun, meaning "who" or "whom."

他是谁 (tā shì shéi)？

When a sentence contains an interrogative pronoun, the particle "吗 (ma)" is typically not used at the end of the sentence.

Example:

Question: 你叫什么名字 (Nǐ jiào shénme míngzi)？ - What's your name? (Literally, "You are called what name?")
Answer: 我叫小美 (Wǒ jiào Xiǎoměi)。 - I am called Xiaomei.
Question: 他是谁 (Tā shì shéi)？ - Who is he? (Literally, "He is who?")
Answer: 他是我的同学 (Tā shì wǒde tóngxué)。 - He is my classmate.

In the above example, the position of 谁 (shéi) in the question is the same as the position of 我的同学 (wǒde tóngxué) in the answer.

Note that 吗 (ma) is not used at the end of sentences that use interrogative pronouns since the interrogative pronouns already fulfill the function of indicating a question.

V. The Possessive Particle 的 (de)

In Chinese, the particle "的 (de)" is placed after a noun or pronoun to indicate possession, similar to the English possessive 's or possessive pronouns (my, your, his, her, etc.).

我 (wǒ)的 (de) 名字 (míngzi) (my name)
同学 (tóngxué)的 (de) 名字 (míngzi) (classmate's name)

Grammar Supplement

Asking questions with 请问 (qǐng wèn)

"请问 (qǐng wèn)" means "May I ask...". "请问 (qǐng wèn)" is an expression of polite request.

Remember to add "请问 (qǐng wèn)" (May I ask...) to be polite when asking someone's name in Chinese.

The verb 叫 (jiào)

The verb "叫 (jiào)" "to call" or "to be called" is used to ask one's full name or given name.

Chinese names 姓名 (xìngmíng)

In Chinese have two parts: the surname (family name) "姓 (xìng)" and the given name "名 (míng)". Chinese names have a different order than in English; the surname comes before the given name. For example, in the dialogue, "吴 (Wú)", "李 (Lǐ)", and "于 (Yú)" are the surnames while "小美 (Xiǎoměi)", "文中 (Wénzhōng)", and "英 (Yīng)" are the given names.
